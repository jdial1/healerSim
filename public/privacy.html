Privacy Policy for Overheal

Last updated: 5 September 2026

Overheal is a single-player game. It does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data. There is no account, no login, no analytics, no advertising, and no crash-reporting SDK.

Data the app stores

Your progress — character level, experience, talent choices, completed dungeons, and action bar layout — is saved on your own device so the game can resume where you left off. Nothing else is recorded.

  • Android app: stored in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Uninstalling the app deletes it.
  • Web version: stored in your browser's local storage. Clearing site data for this site deletes it.

Data sent off your device

None, unless you choose to play with other people. Single player never opens a network connection and works fully in airplane mode; the web version is a static page and sends your progress nowhere.

The Android app now requests INTERNET for the optional public queue, and the Firebase libraries it uses add three more of their own: ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (whether you are online), READ_GSERVICES and a private receiver permission. None of them grants access to your files, contacts, location or anything else personal. With multiplayer switched off, which is the default, none of the multiplayer code runs at all.

Nothing is sent until you turn multiplayer on in Settings and open a dungeon. Then:

  • An anonymous account. The app signs in with Firebase Authentication — no email, no name, no password, just a random id that stays with this installation. Google's Firebase service also records the IP address and app version of each connection, which it uses for security and to prevent abuse.
  • Your place in the queue: that id, the role you are queueing as, the dungeon, and when you joined and were last seen. It is deleted when you leave the queue or your group forms. If the app is closed first, other players' games stop counting it within seconds, and once it has been silent for a minute it is deleted by the next player to use the queue.
  • Your character, to the players in your group: class, level, talent choices and action-bar order, so whoever is running the fight can play your character as you built it.
  • The fight itself: the spells you ask to cast and the state of the encounter, relayed between the players in the group.

There is no chat and no way to send anything else. All of it travels over HTTPS.

Retention, honestly: the record of a group — who was in it, their characters and the last state of the fight — is currently kept on the server after the run ends, and there is not yet a way to ask for it or your anonymous account to be deleted. Both are being added before multiplayer is released.

One indirect exception is worth naming: if you have Android's system backup enabled, Android may copy your saved progress to your own Google account as part of the standard device backup, the same as it does for other apps. That transfer is performed by Android, not by Overheal, and is covered by Google's privacy policy. You can turn it off in your device's backup settings.

Children

Overheal is not directed at children under 13. Single player collects no data from anyone; multiplayer collects only what is described above.
